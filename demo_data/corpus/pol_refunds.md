# Refunds and Timelines

## overview

A refund returns money after Northstar accepts a qualifying return or adjustment. This article is not the return-window article. Being inside 30 calendar days means a return may be requested. It does not mean a card has been credited. The assistant explains when issuance starts, how long a provider may take after issuance, and when a stale issued refund should go to support.

## rules

Northstar initiates a refund after the returned item is received and inspected, unless the order page explicitly offers an earlier refund. A customer saying the refund already happened is not proof. After the store marks the refund issued, the payment provider still has to post it. Card refunds generally take 5 to 10 business days after issuance. Banks and wallets can fall outside that range. The status and date in Orders or Returns override this general estimate.

## stale

If the refund status has not changed for 10 business days after the store marks it issued, the customer should contact support and include the order number. Before those 10 business days have passed, the customer should wait and watch the order page. A refund issued 3 business days ago is still inside the estimate. A refund issued 12 business days ago that still shows no status change has crossed the stale line.

## exceptions

An earlier refund, a different destination card, or a refund before the warehouse receives the item happens only when the order page says so. Gift-card and store-credit outcomes follow the gift-card article. A marketplace seller can add its own review before money moves. A pending authorization that was never captured is not a refund. It is uncaptured payment activity and follows the payment article.

## steps

Read the refund status and the issued date on the order. If no refund is recorded, do not say one is on the way. If it was issued fewer than 10 business days ago, give the 5 to 10 business day card estimate and do not claim it has posted. If it was issued and unchanged for 10 business days or more, direct the customer to support with the order number. Never ask for a full card number, CVV, or one-time passcode.

## examples

Return received, card refund issued 3 business days ago: the provider may still be posting it. Use the 5 to 10 business day estimate. Do not say the money is already on the card.

Refund issued 12 business days ago and still unchanged: the 10 business day stale check has failed. Contact support with the order number.

The customer never shipped the return and the order shows no refund: do not confirm a refund. Issuance normally waits for receipt and inspection.

The customer asks whether this is the same clock as the 30-day return window. It is not. Thirty calendar days govern the return request. Five to 10 business days govern posting after issuance.

## agent_rules

Never claim a refund was issued, posted, or denied from the customer's statement alone. Never shorten the general card estimate to 3 days or extend it past 10 business days. Never treat a return request, or a still-open 30-day window, as a completed refund.
