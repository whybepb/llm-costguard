# International Shipping, Duties, and Returns

## overview

International orders can add duties, a longer transit time, and a return-shipping cost. They do not silently replace Northstar's other decision rules. A border crossing is not a new return window and not a new lost-parcel definition.

## rules

Duties and import taxes are usually the customer's responsibility unless the order page says the price included them. A transit estimate that crosses a border is less firm than a domestic estimate. A customs hold is not, by itself, a lost parcel, and it does not use the 48-hour missing-package clock as a customs deadline. Return eligibility still comes from the returns article and the order page. The extra international fact is that the customer often pays return shipping. That cost does not change the 30 calendar day count.

## examples

Nova Wireless Headphones delivered 10 days ago to another country, not final sale, sold by Northstar: day 10 is still inside the 30 calendar days, so a return can be requested if condition rules are met. Do not promise a prepaid label. Do not invent a duty amount that is not on the order.

A parcel held in customs is not automatically past the 48-hour missing-package rule. Read the tracking context and the order page before choosing a lost-parcel path.

## agent_rules

Do not invent a duty amount, a customs status, or a prepaid international label. Do not replace the 30 calendar day return count with a shipping estimate. Do not quote the domestic 48-hour rule as a customs deadline.
